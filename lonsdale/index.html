The Riding That
Swung the Other Way

In 2024, British Columbia lurched right — Surrey cracked, the Interior hardened, and the NDP majority shrank to a single seat. North Vancouver-Lonsdale did the opposite: every one of its polls moved toward the NDP. This is the anatomy of a counter-swing.

01 · The groundSixty-six polls between mountain and sea

Lonsdale runs uphill from the Burrard Inlet waterfront — the Shipyards and the Quay, up the corridor of mid-rise Central Lonsdale, into the leafier single-family streets below the Upper Levels. Sixty-six voting areas, eleven election-day voting places, about 44,000 registered voters.

Demographically it is the exact profile that resisted BC's 2024 rightward swing: half the adults hold a university degree (50.8%), nearly half rent (47%), the median age is 42, and the average home costs $1.19 million.

Hover any poll for its numbers. Greyed edges on the north and east are territory this riding ceded to Seymour in the 2023 redistribution.

02 · The arcFrom Liberal seat to NDP fortress in three elections

For a decade this was a BC Liberal riding — 44 to 49 per cent through 2005, 2009 and 2013. In 2017 Bowinn Ma flipped it with 45.4 per cent against a divided centre-right, and then the floor gave way: 59.9 per cent in 2020, 64.9 in 2024. The party that held this seat for more than two decades no longer exists on the ballot.

2024 was a pure two-way race — NDP versus Conservative, no Liberal, no Green. Ma won it by 7,686 votes.

04 · The counter-swingAll sixty-six polls moved the same direction

Now compare each poll's election-day vote in 2017 — the year Ma first won — with 2024. While the province swung toward the Conservatives, every single one of Lonsdale's 66 polls swung toward the NDP, by an average of 18.7 points. There is no pocket of resistance, no street that went the province's way. The swing map of this riding is a monochrome.

05 · Every doorNo channel left to lose

A losing party can usually point somewhere — the advance vote, the mail vote, election day. Not here. The NDP won advance voting 66.8 to 33.2, election day 62.7 to 37.3, and the mail vote by better than two-and-a-half to one. However the ballots arrived, the Conservatives never reached two-fifths of the vote.

06 · Who shows upA young riding that actually votes

Provincially, turnout climbs steeply with age — seniors out-vote the young by seventeen points. Lonsdale's gradient is half that: in 2024, 18-to-24-year-olds turned out at 56.3 per cent against 65.3 for 65-to-74s — a nine-point gap, one of the flattest age curves in BC. In a riding where renters and graduates lean NDP, that flat curve is itself part of the majority.

Overall turnout has drifted down from its 2017 peak — 65.7 then, 58.9 in 2024 — mirroring the provincial slide.

07 · What Lonsdale meansThe other pole of the realignment

In The Sorted Province we showed that BC's 2024 swing ran along education and tenure lines: the NDP bled where degrees are scarce and English is a second language, and gained where graduates cluster. Lonsdale is the cleanest specimen of the second kind — the same election that nearly cost the NDP its majority made this riding one of its safest seats.

That is the strategic meaning of a counter-swing: the NDP coalition is not shrinking so much as relocating — out of the suburbs that decide majorities, into ridings like this one that were already won.

Sources & Method

Results. Elections BC official results. Riding-level figures 2005–2024 are actual counts across all voting opportunities. Poll-level maps use the only sub-riding data published: general voting by voting area (2017) and final voting by voting place (2024, attributed to voting-area catchments via Elections BC's official VA→voting-place assignment).

The swing map compares in-person election-day universes only (55% of 2017 votes; 44% of 2024). Both years' advance and absentee ballots are reported at riding level only. Polls altered by the 2023 redistribution use registered-voter-weighted estimates from their 2017 parent polls, per Elections BC's conversion table, and are tagged estimated in tooltips. North Vancouver-Lonsdale ceded territory to North Vancouver-Seymour and received none, so every 2024 poll descends from 2017 Lonsdale territory.

Demographics: 2021 Census (Statistics Canada), FSA profiles weighted to the riding by registered voters per voting area. Turnout: ballots cast ÷ registered voters, Elections BC. Age participation: Elections BC voter participation by age group.

These are area-level associations; they do not establish individual behaviour.
