1 · Where you are

The valley that picks governments

Courtenay, Comox, CFB Comox and the rural north of the Comox Valley — one of BC's oldest ridings by age, its biggest retirement destination per capita, and its most reliable cliff-hanger. Every shape is a voting area, coloured by its polling place's 2024 election-day count.

Blue is Conservative Brennan Day. Orange is two-term NDP incumbent Ronna-Rae Leonard. The margin, after every ballot: 93 votes.

Election-day ballots on the map; advance and mail ballots count in every riding-wide number we quote — and in this riding they were the majority of all votes.

2 · 2017: the 189

The seat that made Horgan premier

Election night 2017: Leonard led by nine votes. The recount said 13. The absentee count settled it at 189 — and that one seat denied Christy Clark her 44th, made the Green agreement possible, and put John Horgan in the premier's office.

Remember the shape of that win: NDP 37.4, Liberal 36.7, Green 18.4. This riding elects governments with pluralities, not majorities.

3 · The structural fifth

One in five, every single time

Same map, recoloured by the 2024 Green vote. Green took 18.4% here in 2017, 19.6 in 2020, 20.7 in 2024 — three different candidates, three different climates, same fifth of the electorate. In 2024 that was Arzeena Hamir's 7,202 votes.

Day won with 38.8% — twelve points below the NDP's own 2020 result. He didn't beat the centre-left. He waited for it to divide.

4 · The twin towns

Where the last week was lost

Courtenay's election day broke 41.1–35.5 for Day; Comox 38.3–37.7. But the NDP won the advance vote riding-wide, 40.0 to 38.0. Late deciders broke right — the same final-week pattern that decided the federal seat. The campaign didn't lose the valley; it lost the last ten days.

6 · The seniors' riding

Day's portfolio is this riding's mirror

Median age 51. Nearly two-thirds of its neighbourhoods are retiree and empty-nest segments; its seniors vote at 78% — near the highest in BC. The hospital in Courtenay has run over capacity since the year it opened. And Brennan Day — local businessman, second-time candidate — is now the Conservatives' critic for Seniors and Rural Health.

Whoever owns seniors' care, home support and family-doctor attachment owns this riding.

7 · The whole picture

He didn't win the valley. The split did.

Put it back together: Day 13,481, Leonard 13,388, Hamir 7,202. The NDP fell twelve points from its 2020 high while the Green fifth stood still and the right consolidated. Forty-seven Green voters switching — about one in a hundred and fifty of them — flips the seat back.

Leonard, after two terms, is retiring territory now; the 2028 race starts from an open NDP nomination against a four-year incumbent. The clock matters.

What actually happened here

Not a realignment — a split. The Conservative share (38.8%) sits twelve points below the NDP's own 2020 result. What changed is that the anti-Conservative vote divided cleanly in two while the right unified. The provincial mirror of the federal seat, with Greens playing the Liberals' role.

vote-splitting, n.

When two parties divide the voters who oppose a third, and the third wins with a minority. Courtenay–Comox 2024: NDP 38.6 + Green 20.7 = 59.3% of the riding voting for someone other than Brennan Day — who is the MLA.

What to do about it · October 21, 2028

The Beat-Day Playbook

The arithmetic, one more time — this seat has been decided by fewer than 200 votes twice in eight years.

1

Name the split, gently, from day one

Day holds this seat with 38.8% — twelve points under the NDP's 2020 share — because one voter in five goes Green here, every time. The memory is the message: 189 votes made John Horgan premier; 93 votes made Brennan Day. Forty-seven Green switchers flip 2028.

Squeeze the marginal Green — the 2020 Green-curious NDP voter — and never scold the committed one. Co-opt: watershed protection, the Comox Lake drinking-water catchment, the estuary file.

2

Own the seniors' portfolio before the Seniors critic does

This is BC's retirement riding: two-thirds retiree neighbourhoods, median age 51, senior turnout near the provincial top — and Day literally holds the Seniors and Rural Health critic file. The NDP candidate must be credible on the over-capacity hospital, long-term-care beds, home support and family-doctor attachment — in Comox Valley numbers, not provincial talking points. Security-seeking retirees decided this seat; speak security.

3

Fix the last ten days

The NDP won the 2024 advance vote 40.0–38.0 and lost election day — late deciders broke right, exactly as they did federally. Structure 2028 to peak late: hold back a signature local announcement, and build an election-day machine to match the advance one.

4

Defend the rural north, court the base

Black Creek and Merville moved +14 and crossed 50% right — the valley's edge is realigning like the resource belt one riding north. The defensive plays are material: agricultural water security after the drought years, rural roads and internet, and respect-forward engagement at CFB Comox, where federal Liberals took three in ten votes — persuadable centrists, not Conservatives.

5

Recruit the successor now

Leonard won this seat twice and is done. An open nomination against an incumbent with four years of profile is the campaign's biggest risk. The profile that fits: a Comox Valley healthcare or local-government figure with seniors-care credibility, named by early 2027, with eighteen months to replace what Leonard's retirement takes away.

6

Make Day answer for his party, since he keeps distancing himself

Day publicly disowned his colleague's letter defending Aaron Gunn's residential-school comments. Good — make him keep disowning: the Rustad ouster, the OneBC split, the direction his own departing colleague called "Trump-style populism." Every week Day spends differentiating himself from his party, he isn't defining the NDP candidate. In a 93-vote seat, discomfort is arithmetic.

7

Run the two ridings as one campaign

Same media market, same hospital catchment, same federal overlap — and the federal beat-Gunn squeeze targets the same Comox Valley middle. Shared organizers, shared research, shared events with North Island: two 2024 losses by a combined 732 votes should be one 2028 operation.
