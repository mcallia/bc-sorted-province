West Vancouver-Capilano · 2005–2024

The Seat That
Wouldn't Turn

Lonsdale fell to the NDP in 2017. Seymour fell in 2020. Capilano never fell — not in the realignment, not in the landslide. In 2024 the party that held it left the field, its incumbent ran alone as an independent, and the seat still wouldn't turn: it simply changed uniforms. This is what the wall looks like, poll by poll.

01 · The groundSixty-five polls above the Lions Gate

Capilano climbs from the Park Royal waterfront through Ambleside and Sentinel Hill into the British Properties, and takes in the Capilano flats on the North Vancouver side of the river. Sixty-five voting areas, fourteen election-day voting places, about 44,000 registered voters.

It is the most extreme riding in this series: the average dwelling is worth $2.52 million, the median age is 48 — a quarter of residents are over 65 — 70 per cent of households own, and nearly a quarter speak a language other than English at home. It also holds the North Shore's highest share of university graduates, at 60 per cent: on the Sorted Province's education map, this should be contested ground.

02 · The arcTwo-thirds, three elections running

The BC Liberals took 68.3, 67.5 and 67.0 per cent here in 2005, 2009 and 2013. In 2017, as Lonsdale fell, Capilano held at 57.2. In 2020, in the biggest NDP landslide in BC history, it held again — 53.6 — one of only 28 seats in the province that did, and among the safest of them. The NDP's ceiling here, in its best year ever, was 30.3 per cent.

Then 2024: the BC Liberals' successor party withdrew from the race province-wide, incumbent Karin Kirkpatrick ran as an independent and took 20.6 per cent — and Conservative Lynne Block won anyway, 46.7 to the NDP's 27.1. The BC Liberals never lost Capilano. They simply ceased to exist.

03 · 2024, up closeThirteen blue places — and one beachhead

Shade each poll by its election-day NDP share and Capilano is the negative image of its neighbours: pale everywhere, the Conservatives first at thirteen of fourteen voting places. The exception is the riding's south-east corner: at Lions Gate Community Recreation Centre, on the Capilano flats beside the bridge, the NDP edged the Conservatives 40.5 to 40.0 — a half-point beachhead in a sea of blue.

04 · The divided swingThe flats moved left. The Properties moved right.

Here Capilano breaks from its neighbours. Lonsdale's polls swung NDP 66-for-66; Seymour's 69-of-70. Capilano's map is split: 49 polls up, 16 down, a mean of just +3.3. And the geography is mostly legible: the NDP's deepest retreats stack up upslope — around Sentinel Secondary and Westcot in the British Properties, its share fell as much as 10.9 points, to lows near 12 per cent — while its strongest advances came at West Vancouver Community Centre on the Ambleside flats (+15.7) and École Cleveland in Edgemont (+12.7).

05 · Kirkpatrick countryWhere the old party's vote went personal

Karin Kirkpatrick held this seat for the BC Liberals-turned-BC United. When the party stood down and she ran alone, one voter in five stayed with her. Shade the polls by her share and the old Liberal map glows faintly back: her vote ran between — and — per cent by voting place, strongest at —.

Add her vote to the Conservative column and the combined right reaches 67 per cent — almost exactly the BC Liberal share of 2013. The coalition didn't shrink. It briefly ran under two names.

06 · Every doorBlue by every route

The Conservatives won every voting channel — the advance vote 48.8 to the NDP's 25.9, election day 43.8 to 27.1, the mail vote 41.8 to 34.5. Kirkpatrick ran stronger on election day (23.0) than in the advance (20.0); it changed nothing. No channel offered the NDP a path, or the independent a miracle.

07 · Who shows upThe oldest electorate, the widest gap

Capilano's turnout is a gerontocracy even by North Shore standards: 68.6 per cent among 65-to-74s against 42.6 for 25-to-34s — a 26-point chasm, the widest in this series. Overall turnout, 58.6 per cent, is eight points below Seymour's: wealth here does not translate into participation, because the young and the renters who might change this seat largely don't vote in it.

08 · What Capilano meansThe wall, and its one crack

Across The Sorted Province we saw education pull ridings toward the NDP and ownership pull them right. Seymour showed the diploma beating the deed. Capilano is the reverse case — the North Shore's best-educated riding, still unturned — because here the deed comes with a median age of 48, a quarter-senior electorate, and a 26-point turnout gap that silences the voters who might have flipped it.

Three ridings, three verdicts: Lonsdale is the realignment's heartland, Seymour its frontier — and Capilano its wall. But walls erode from the base: the one place the NDP took, by half a point, is the youngest, densest corner of the riding. The crack is small. It is also exactly where every crack in this series has started.

Sources & Method

Results. Elections BC official results. Riding-level figures 2005–2024 are actual counts across all voting opportunities (2009 and 2013 reported under the era's riding code, WCA). Poll-level maps use the only sub-riding data published: general voting by voting area (2017) and final voting by voting place (2024, attributed to voting-area catchments via Elections BC's official VA→voting-place assignment).

The swing map compares in-person election-day universes only (58% of 2017 votes; 40% of 2024). Both years' advance and absentee ballots are reported at riding level only. Polls altered by the 2023 redistribution use registered-voter-weighted estimates from their 2017 parent polls, per Elections BC's conversion table; four polls transferred in from West Vancouver-Sea to Sky draw some or all of their 2017 baselines from that riding. Seven site-based voting areas (care facilities and similar) have no map geography and are excluded from the poll count. The 2024 field was four-way; all shares are of the full field.

Demographics: 2021 Census (Statistics Canada), FSA profiles weighted to the riding by registered voters per voting area. Turnout: ballots cast ÷ registered voters, Elections BC. Age participation: Elections BC voter participation by age group.

These are area-level associations; they do not establish individual behaviour.
