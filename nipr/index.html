The riding that didn't move — and flipped anyway

In April 2025, Aaron Gunn took a seat New Democrats had held for a decade — by 4,999 votes. Scroll through all 258 polls and watch what actually happened: his vote barely grew. The vote against him split. Scroll — we'll walk the map.

1 · Where you are

Half an island, a working coast

North Island–Powell River runs from the Comox Valley to Cape Scott, plus the mainland coast around Powell River — mill towns, fishing harbours, logging settlements, and the homelands of more than twenty First Nations. Rachel Blaney won it for the NDP three times.

Every shape on this map is one polling division, coloured by who won it in 2025. Blue is Gunn. Orange is NDP. Red is Liberal.

Darker colour = bigger local margin. Advance and mail ballots are folded into their home polls, so every counted vote is on this map.

2 · What changed

The wave came from third place

Same map, recoloured: red where the Liberal share rose. It rose nearly everywhere — a party that took 13% here in 2021 took 26% in 2025, without a local campaign to speak of.

249of 258 polls where the Liberal share rose
11.7median Liberal gain, points per poll

This was the national Carney surge landing on a riding no Liberal has ever won — not a local swing to Gunn.

3 · Campbell River

His biggest town barely moved

Campbell River is the riding's population centre and its aquaculture capital — the open-net fish-farm phase-out has real payrolls attached here. If a Conservative surge was coming anywhere, it was coming here.

It didn't come. Gunn's share crept from 41.7 to 43.8. The NDP lost six and a half points — and the Liberals took eleven.

11.5LIB gain in the same polls

23,365 votes counted here in 2025 — NDP candidate Tanille Johnston's home council seat, and still the NDP fell to 28.8%.

4 · Courtenay–Comox

The new ground decided it

The valley end of the riding — Courtenay, Comox, the rural north — is its biggest block: 30,415 votes, grown for 2025 when redistribution pushed the boundary deeper into Courtenay. CFB Comox, retirees, commuters: the most Liberal-friendly terrain on this map.

Here the wave peaked — Liberals up 17 points. And here is the tell: Gunn's own share went down, 37.1 to 36.1, in the very polls that made him an MP.

5 · Powell River

The one place that truly broke

Now the map shows Conservative change — and the only deep-blue patch on the coast. Powell River lost the Catalyst Tis'kwat mill for good in 2023, a century-old employer, about 200 union jobs at the end and the tax base with it.

The NDP fell nearly eleven points here; Gunn rose eleven. Town polls that went 47% NDP in 2021 landed at a 36.8 – 36.8 dead heat.

10.9CON gain in Powell River, points
200jobs at Tis'kwat when the closure was made permanent

Texada and the coast road swung with the mill — CON +15 on the island that barged its rock to the plant.

6 · The resource belt

Where the grievance is real

Gold River, Tahsis, Zeballos, Port Alice, Woss, Sayward — mill and logging villages where the coastal harvest has fallen more than 40% since 2019, thousands of jobs gone since 2022, and every curtailment has names attached. This is the other place Gunn genuinely gained: up five in the west-coast villages, up four in Sayward.

But count the votes: all of it together is smaller than one Campbell River suburb. The grievance is real. The numbers are not where the seat was lost.

7 · The wall

Where the vote got stronger

North of Campbell River the map stays orange. Port Hardy, Port McNeill, Sointula, Alert Bay, the Broughton communities: the NDP held its towns while the province swung right — and in First Nations communities the wall got taller.

Gunn kept his candidacy through a firestorm over his residential-school comments. These polls answered: Alert Bay and the Broughton went two-thirds NDP, up 14 points. Tsulquate, up 27, reached 75%. Cape Mudge 74%.

67.4NDP % in Alert Bay & Broughton polls, 2025
14points gained there since 2021

Quadra and Cortes did the same arithmetic differently: the Green vote came home and the islands went 59% NDP.

8 · The whole picture

He didn't win the riding. The split did.

Put it back together. Turnout jumped, and roughly 12,000 more votes were counted than the 2021 tally adjusted to the new boundaries. The NDP's own vote held — 561 short of 2021. Nearly everything new went Liberal, and the PPC's 4.6% folded into Gunn's column.

Gunn finished at 38.7%. Six in ten voters in this riding voted against him — and he's the MP.

What actually happened here

This wasn't a riding lurching right. It was a three-way race breaking over a two-party seat — the national Liberal surge cut the opposition to Gunn in half, and 38.7% was suddenly enough to win.

vote-splitting, n.

When two parties divide the voters who oppose a third, and the third wins with a minority of the vote. In 2025: NDP 32.6% + Liberal 26.2% + Green 1.9% = 60.7% of this riding voting for someone other than Aaron Gunn.

Held: NDP base — 26,357 votes (−561) First Nations strongholds — up to 89% NDP Gunn's ceiling — ~39%
Gone: Green vote — 5.8% → 1.9% PPC — 4.6% → 0.4% (into Gunn's column) Powell River as a safe NDP town

The rematch arithmetic

4,999 votes. 21,218 Liberals.

No Liberal has ever won this seat, and none will next time. If one in four of those Liberal voters concludes the real contest here is NDP-or-Gunn, the seat flips back — before a single mind on the right is changed. That case gets made town by town, poll by poll. Start with the map.
